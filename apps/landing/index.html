Install

Add DealGuard to Chrome

The extension asks for no browser permissions. It is not on the Chrome Web Store yet, so you load it yourself in three steps.

Download Chrome extension
  1. Unzip dealguard-extension.zip to a folder you will keep.
  2. Open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode.
  3. Choose Load unpacked, pick the unzipped folder, then pin DealGuard from the puzzle-piece menu.
What it looks for

Seven patterns behind most fake deals

The three in coral are hard stops: any one makes the verdict Likely scam on its own. The rest add points. The examples are illustrations, not real messages.

Hard stop

Pay first

Asks you to pay a fee, deposit or courier charge before any work starts.

Password, OTP or card

How it decides

Code decides. A model only explains.

Every message becomes a list of checks. Each check is fired, clear or unknown, and unknown never counts as a pass. A plain scoring rule turns the list into a verdict. Claude can explain the result after it is set, but it can't change it.

  • Hard stop Likely scam, whatever the points
  • 60 or more Likely scam
  • 30 to 59 Suspicious
  • Under 30 No red flags found
Points per check

Scale 0 to 40 points. Hard stops are not shown because they decide the verdict on their own. Checks that need a network lookup show as unknown in the demo.

Privacy

Your messages stay with you

On your device

  • The checks and the score
  • Your saved messages and history
  • No account and no server to sign up for

Only if you switch it on

  • Explain with AI: the message text goes to the DealGuard server and Claude only when you press the button. Emails, phone numbers, card and ID numbers, UPI IDs, link paths, handles and most names are removed first, and nothing is stored
  • Domain age lookup: the sender's domain goes to the registry (planned)
  • Gmail connect: read-only, unread mail from people not in your contacts (planned)

DealGuard never says a message is safe. It says what it found and what it couldn't check.
